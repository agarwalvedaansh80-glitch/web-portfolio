What to text a customer when the tech is running late

A late arrival is forgivable. A late arrival with no message is not. Four short texts that keep customers on your side.

JP
Jonah Pratt
Customer support, Slotted
Oct 7, 20263 min read

Customers plan their day around your arrival window. They work from home that morning, they move the car, they wait in. When the window slips and nobody tells them, the frustration is not about the twenty minutes. It is about being left to guess.

Send the update before they notice

The best time to tell a customer you are late is before the original window starts. A message at 9:40 that says the new window is 10:20 to 10:50 feels like good service. The same message at 10:15 feels like an excuse.

Four texts worth keeping as templates

  • On the way: "Ana from Northside Heating is on her way. Arriving 9:20 to 9:50."
  • Running late: "Ana is finishing an earlier job. Your new window is 10:20 to 10:50. Reply 1 to keep it or 2 to rebook."
  • Nearly there: "Ana is about 6 minutes away."
  • Rebooked: "You are booked for Thursday, 8:00 to 8:30. We will text you when Ana is on the way."

Each one says who is coming, when, and what the customer can do next. None of them apologises for more than one sentence or explains the traffic.

Give them a way out

Some customers cannot wait an extra hour. Offering a one-reply rebook turns a complaint into a scheduling change. It also frees the slot for a customer who can take it.

People forgive a late van. They do not forgive a silent one.
