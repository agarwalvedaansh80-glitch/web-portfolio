From phone call to closed job

Four steps, one board. The office sees every van, the techs see their day, and the customer always knows when to expect the knock on the door.

1

A job comes in

Calls, web forms and repeat maintenance visits land in one list, with the address, equipment and urgency already filled in.

2

Slotted suggests the slot

It checks who holds the right certification, who is closest and where the day has room. Accept with one tap, or drag the job somewhere else.

3

The tech and the customer both know

The tech’s phone shows the route and the job notes. The customer gets a text with a 30-minute arrival window, and another if it moves.

4

The job closes on site

Photos, parts used and a signature go straight back to the office, so the invoice is ready before the van pulls away.

Built for days that don’t go to plan

Overruns, sick days, a part that never arrived. Slotted rearranges the board around them and tells the people affected.

When a job runs long, the day moves with it

Slotted pushes the next visits back, checks they still fit before the end of the shift, and texts each customer a new window. Nobody has to ring round.

Arrival windows that hold

Windows come from live drive times and narrow as the tech gets closer, so nobody waits in all morning.

Skills before distance

Tag certifications once. Slotted never sends the nearest person if they are not qualified for the job.

Works in basements
